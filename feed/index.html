The last glacial cycle in western Norway—extensive MIS 5d glaciation, multiple glacial advances and LGM glacial advance phases

Eiliv A. Larsen, Hans Petter Sejrup, BRENT C. WARD, William E. N. Austin, Hilary H. Birks, Oddvar Longva, … (+4)
Boreas · 2026-10-22
53.0 matched: ice sheet, ice flow, glacier, deglaciation, last glacial maximum, LGM, glacial cycle, MIS stage, foraminifera, radiocarbon, sea level
abstract

We have investigated fine‐grained marine sediments buried under delta sediments and multiple till beds in a tributary valley in western Norway. The marine bed contains bivalves, foraminifera and terrestrial plant material. The fauna and flora are rather poor, but the presence of molluscan species Mytilus edulis and Arctica islandica suggests that Atlantic waters reached the coastal areas at the time of deposition. The age range of radiocarbon dates from about 25 to 50 ka BP conflicts with previous studies from the area and is considered as minimum ages only. This conclusion is supported by amino acid epimerization values in molluscs from the radiocarbon‐dated unit, indicating that it should be correlated with an Early Weichselian, MIS 5c interstadial. The marine unit and overlying delta succession are situated on the outer coast up to 56 m above present sea level, suggesting that they were deposited during a high sea‐level stand following retreat of an extensive MIS 5d glaciation. A compiled Weichselian glaciation curve shows five major glacial advances extending to the outermost coast or beyond. These advances seem to correspond to periods with North Atlantic sea surface cooling. Three till units on top of the delta are interpreted to represent glacier advance prior to the Last Glacial Maximum (LGM) and during the subsequent deglaciation. Clast fabrics in the lower till and orientation of clastic dikes show an upvalley component opposite to LGM maximum ice‐flow direction. This indicates that initial glacier advance was by fjord glaciers extending to the outer coast while there was limited glacier extent in coastal mountain areas. This interpretation favours fast ice flow along the fjords and major supply of ice from interior mountain areas. Glacier growth in coastal mountains led to merging with fjord glaciers, reorientation of the ice‐flow direction and ultimately maximum glacier extent. Empirical data on ice‐sheet growth are extremely rare and may have been overlooked in many stratigraphic records. Such data are vital for understanding behaviour of ice sheets and in a wider sense, the climate system.

Reversible Atlantic overturning despite continued Greenland ice sheet melt

Chuncheng Guo, Shuting Yang, Ilana Schiller‐Weiss, Jorge A. Bernales, Steffen M. Olsen, Torben Koenigk, … (+3)
Science Advances · 2026-10-14 · cites-me
50.0 matched: ice sheet, Greenland, meltwater, mass loss, overshoot, irreversible, Atlantic meridional overturning circulation, AMOC, overturning circulation, sea level, sea-level rise, Earth system model, Earth system, global warming
abstract

The Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) is considered vulnerable to irreversible weakening or collapse under global warming and related Greenland ice sheet (GrIS) melt, yet its resilience to either forcing remains poorly constrained. Here, we use a carbon dioxide emission-driven Earth system model with an interactive GrIS to assess AMOC reversibility under a range of idealized carbon dioxide emission pathways that produce near-linear global warming up to 10 kelvins (K), stabilization across 1.5 to 9 K, and subsequent cooling. We find that although the AMOC reaches threshold-defined collapsed states, these do not represent dynamical tipping in this model: The overturning weakens quasi-linearly with global mean temperature increase yet consistently and promptly recovers under cooling. In contrast, GrIS mass loss accelerates with warming, continues through stabilizations, and is only slowed by cooling, committing the planet to long-term sea level rise. These results reveal contrasting resilience across climate system components: Under transient carbon dioxide forcing, AMOC strength remains dynamically reversible despite continued Greenland meltwater input, whereas the GrIS is locked into persistent decline. Our findings underscore the urgency of rapid emission cuts to limit climate overshoot, AMOC weakening, and irreversible ice sheet loss.

abstract

The Weddell Sea influences global ocean circulation and sea level through modulating Antarctic Bottom Water formation and Antarctic Ice Sheet discharges, but the history of ice‐sheet dynamics in this region remains poorly constrained due to limited well‐dated geological records. To provide a new geochemical perspective on ice‐sheet dynamics since the Last Glacial Maximum (LGM), this study presents paired authigenic and detrital Pb isotope records from sediment core PS1599‐3 recovered from the continental slope north of the Filchner Trough. Comparison with Antarctic Pb isotope provinces indicates that both detrital and authigenic Pb signatures were possibly sourced from a Shackleton Range‐like region in the south‐eastern Weddell Sea sector of East Antarctica. This suggests that dominant upstream drainage sources remained broadly stable throughout the last ~23 ka. We interpret the downcore Pb isotope variations to primarily reflect changes in the delivery of subglacial weathering‐derived Pb associated with grounding‐line dynamics in the Filchner Trough. Authigenic 206 Pb/ 204 Pb ratios show a sharp increase at ~23 ka, followed by two decreases at ~20 and 11–9 ka. We suggest that a re‐advance of grounded ice near the LGM was followed by initial retreat at ~20 ka and further grounding‐line retreat or reorganization during the Early Holocene. These findings highlight the use of authigenic Pb isotopes as potential tracers for reconstructing polar ice‐sheet dynamics and meltwater influences.

The role of upper–deep ocean heat exchange in shaping warming patterns and climate feedbacks in CMIP6 models

Guo Duan, Duoying Ji, Min Cui, 吕欣宇, Jiaxu Wang, Yongjiu Dai
Journal of Climate · 2026-10-28
37.0 matched: energy balance model, Atlantic meridional overturning circulation, AMOC, overturning circulation, Southern Ocean, millennial-scale, CMIP, climate feedback, CO2, ensemble
abstract

Abstract Surface warming patterns modulate climate feedbacks. Ocean heat exchange redistributes heat between the upper and deep ocean, shaping warming patterns. However, its influence on climate feedbacks remains unclear. We explore this issue in the CMIP6 abrupt-4×CO2 experiment by dividing the simulation into fast, intermediate and slow phases and using an effective upper–deep ocean heat exchange coefficient (γ) inferred from a two-layer energy balance model. The intermediate phase stands out as the period when ocean heat exchange leaves its strongest imprint on warming patterns and climate feedbacks. Models with stronger ocean heat exchange (high-γ ensemble) differ from those with weaker exchange (low-γ ensemble) in both background ocean state and response, exhibiting weaker mean-state stratification and a stronger Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) favoring stronger heat penetration, subsurface warming, and distinct warming pattern and feedback evolution. Across the three phases, the low-γ ensemble sustains intensified warming in the tropical eastern Pacific and Southern Ocean alongside subdued warming in the Northern Hemisphere extratropics. However, the high-γ ensemble experiences a warming pattern reversal over these regions, tied to stronger intermediate-phase heat penetration and AMOC weakening. This centennial-scale warming pattern reversal, overlooked in previous work, partly mirrors the millennial-scale reversal previously found in multi-millennial abrupt-4×CO2 simulations. The high-γ ensemble also exhibits a larger increase and later peak change in net climate feedback. These findings highlight ocean heat exchange and associated ocean mean-state differences as key controls on time-evolving warming patterns and climate feedbacks.

abstract

During interglaciations, the Laurentide Ice Sheet (LIS), which is the largest of the Pleistocene ice sheets, retreats to Arctic Canada, where it achieves a minimum configuration. The reexpansion of the LIS following this minimum marks the start of the next glaciation. Here, we show that the last remaining remnant of the most recent LIS, Barnes Ice Cap (Baffin Island, Canada), achieved its minimum interglacial dimensions after ~3.6 thousand years ago, before reexpanding ~2000 years ago, marking the onset of the next glaciation. The new LIS reached maximum dimensions in ~1960 CE, after which anthropogenic summer warming resulted in a negative mass balance, ice margin recession, and the termination of the next glaciation. The demise of the next glaciation, despite the ongoing decline in summer insolation, illustrates the influence of anomalous greenhouse gas forcing on climate.

Holocene millennial climate variability influenced by tropical–Antarctic ocean–atmosphere interactions

Peng Zhou, Zhengguo Shi, Zhisheng An, Wenju Cai, Pascale Braconnot, Qiong Zhang
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences · 2026-10-27
26.0 matched: Antarctic, albedo, sea ice, Southern Ocean, sea surface temperature, millennial-scale, Holocene, proxy, transient simulation
abstract

A prominent unstable feature, millennial-scale climate oscillation (MCO), punctuated the Holocene. Solar activity and deep ocean variability are generally regarded as the origin of the MCO, yet the dynamics remain debated. Using a Holocene transient simulation with the EC-Earth climate model (8 to 0 ka BP), together with a global compilation of 304 temperature reconstructions, here we identify a robust ~1,350-y oscillatory mode that characterizes Holocene climate variability and is self-sustained and crucially influenced by tropical–Antarctic ocean–atmosphere interactions. Within each cycle, Antarctic Bottom Water (AABW) strengthening evolves with the developing warming and an El Niño-like tropical sea surface temperature (SST) response, accompanied by anticyclonic circulation anomalies over the high-latitude Southern Ocean. Northward cold advection of the associated enhanced westerly winds off the Antarctic coast overcomes the effect of sea ice retreat to promote AABW formation, ocean stratification, and warming. Positive feedbacks including sea ice-albedo, tropical air–sea interaction, and SST-AABW magnify surface warming till AABW reaches its peak. Thereafter, reduced brine rejection from sea-ice retreat weakens AABW, counteracts the warming, and commences a switch to its cooling phase. The dominant periodicity, consistent with that inferred from proxy reconstructions, is confirmed by complementary climate simulations. These results provide an alternative physical mechanism for Holocene millennial-scale climate variability. Under future anthropogenic warming, a positive phase of the oscillation may heighten the positive feedbacks, thereby increasing the risk of rapid climate transformation.

abstract

Abstract Paleoenvironmental reconstructions using sediments from isolation basins including lakes are useful approaches to obtain evidence of sea-level and ice-sheet changes in Antarctica. However, a detailed study using multiple sediment cores of how isolation basins respond to external forcings is needed to improve the accuracy of paleoenvironmental reconstructions. We reconstructed how the water mass structure of Lake Nurume, located in Langhovde, Lützow-Holm Bay, East Antarctica, changed in response to sea-level and ice-sheet changes during the Holocene using sediments from different water depths and nearby trenches. Sedimentary observations, diatom analyses, and X-ray fluorescence core scanner analyses were conducted to reconstruct the three-dimensional characterization of water mass structure combined with the paleotopographic reconstruction using glacial isostatic adjustment modelling. Sedimentation resumed at approximately 6.6 cal kyr BP when the lake became ice-free and marine conditions, as evidenced by the presence of marine diatoms and shell fossils. The resulting stratification caused anoxic conditions in the bottom water. Finally, at approximately 0.5 cal kyr BP, the lake became completely isolated from the sea, establishing its current water mass structure. These findings contribute to more accurate paleoenvironmental reconstructions such as sea-level and ice-sheet changes using sediments from isolation basins in Antarctica.

The overshoot integral can inform climate science and policy beyond 1.5 °C of warming

James G Dyke, Jesse F. Abrams, John Schellnhuber
Environmental Research Letters · 2026-10-16 · cites-me
20.0 matched: glaciology, glacier, overshoot, permafrost, Earth system, CMIP
abstract

Abstract The carbon budget framework has served as the dominant organising principle for climate science and policy, linking cumulative emissions to peak warming and providing a tractable scalar constraint for international negotiations. With 1.5 °C exceedance now imminent and virtually all mitigation scenarios involving substantial temperature overshoot before net-negative emissions could return temperatures to target, the budget framework’s utility for evaluating and minimising risk generated by warming risks becoming diminished. We propose a complementary organising principle: the overshoot integral, defined as the time-integrated temperature exceedance above a policy-relevant threshold, with units of °C·years. Analogous to degree-heating weeks in coral bleaching prediction and positive degree-days in glaciology, the overshoot integral captures both the magnitude and duration of exceedance in a single scalar quantity. Applying this metric to future climate pathways, we show that pathways with similar peak temperatures can differ by more than two orders of magnitude in their cumulative degree-year burden. Applied to CMIP7 ScenarioMIP/FaIR (Finite Amplitude Impulse Response simple climate model) trajectories, calculating overshoot integrals reveal substantial differences in cumulative thermal exposure across pathways. For Earth-system impacts whose response dynamics integrate forcing over time, spanning marine habitat, permafrost carbon and mountain glaciers, we show that cumulative thermal exposure captured by the overshoot integral contains impact-relevant information not captured by peak warming alone. A simple risk formulation combining peak and cumulative exposure improves out-of-sample prediction across all three systems. That the overshoot integral is best understood not as a reversible account that can be debited and credited, but as a cumulative dose should inform climate policy and science in the increasingly warmer future.

The uncertain impacts of an 1.5 °C overshoot-and-decline scenario on carbon stocks and productivity in terrestrial ecosystems

Laura E. Boeschoten, S J De Hertog, G Recour, Félicien Meunier, J Bitton, H Blondeel, … (+25)

abstract

Abstract Overshoot scenarios, in which global temperatures peak and then decline through emission reductions and CO 2 removal, are increasingly central to climate policy as we are already approaching 1.5 °C warming. Yet the ecological reversibility of such pathways remains poorly understood, even though the trajectories of productivity and carbon stocks in terrestrial ecosystems have major implications for land-based carbon sink projections. Here we draw on four complementary lines of research to study the land carbon sink under overshoot: (i) CMIP6 models project declines in net ecosystem productivity (NEP) after peak warming. Nevertheless, NEP remains positive, leading to a stabilisation of the carbon stocks in terrestrial vegetation (cVeg). The CO 2 ramp up-down model experiment by CDRMIP underscores that these models predict near-reversibility of the vegetation carbon stock. (ii) A systematic review on global change experiments identified 256 papers (warming, drought, irrigation or elevated CO 2 , all elements of an overshoot scenario). However, only 15 included a recovery phase following treatment. This reveals a gap in experimental design for understanding ecosystem responses under overshoot conditions. Where recovery was assessed, responses were mixed, with persistent legacy effects documented after drought. (iii) Modern-day overshoot analogues, including two El Niño events and the 2018–2020 European droughts, show that compound heat and drought can cause carbon losses that recover slowly, with repeated extremes progressively reducing ecosystem resilience. (iv) Paleoclimate records from the Holocene Climatic Optimum and the Paleocene–Eocene Thermal Maximum demonstrate that exceeding ecological thresholds can trigger vegetation changes and carbon cycle feedbacks that long outlast the climatic perturbation itself. Together, these findings show that the trajectory of the terrestrial carbon sink during climate overshoot is uncertain, because ecosystem responses are often delayed, nonlinear, and threshold dependent.

Workshop report: Lake KINneret Drilling (KIND) – reconstructing Pliocene–Quaternary hydroclimate and seismic history of the Levant

Adi Torfstein, Eshly Aizenshtat-Soffer, Moshe Armon, Catherine Beck, S. L. Goldstein, Hannah Hartung, … (+17)
Scientific Drilling · 2026-10-29 new
19.0 matched: Pliocene, Quaternary, sediment core, climate change, global warming
abstract

The southern Levant extends between the hyperarid Sahara–Arabia Desert belt in the south and the temperate Mediterranean climate zone in the north, making the regional hydroclimate extremely sensitive to even small perturbations of global climate. The region further served as a major pathway for the “out-of-Africa” dispersal of fauna, flora, and hominins throughout the Quaternary, as well as for human cultural development from the Paleolithic to the Neolithic. Spanning the length of the Levant is the Dead Sea Transform (DST) that transfers the opening at the Red Sea in the south to the collision at the Taurus Mountains in the north. Pull-apart basins along the DST host the hypersaline Dead Sea and freshwater Lake Kinneret, whose sedimentary records document the regional hydroclimate and seismic history. Lake Kinneret (a.k.a. the Sea of Galilee) is the largest freshwater lake in the Levant, whose precursors have deposited a thick sequence of lacustrine sediments since the late Miocene. Seismic profiles across the tectonically subsiding basin in the middle of the modern lake, as well as existing short sediment cores, indicate that the sedimentary sequences are generally bounded by two N–S-striking longitudinal faults and generally maintain stratigraphic order. The sedimentary column is dominated by primary authigenic calcite, whose chemical and isotopic composition documents regional hydrology and lacustrine conditions from at least the Pliocene. Accordingly, the sedimentary record of Lake Kinneret holds the potential to serve as one of the longest and most highly resolved lacustrine records since the Pliocene, yielding sub-centennial records of hydroclimate, paleoseismicity, geomagnetism, and paleobotanics in a region of fundamental importance for the global understanding of climate change, tectonics, and human evolution. The Lake KINneret Drilling (KIND) project aims to establish an exceptionally long Pliocene–Quaternary chronostratigraphic reference record that will contribute to a meridional synthesis of continental and marine core records along the East African Rift–Dead Sea Transform (EARDST) corridor. Owing to its location, the KIND record will yield transformative contributions to climate modeling and to our understanding of climate change in the eastern Mediterranean, a recognized global warming “hotspot”. Moreover, it will document seismic activity along the DST over long timescales, providing a basis for policymakers to prepare for this potential major hazard to infrastructure and human life. Taken together, the KIND project will provide unique and much-needed records from a tectonically active region that is projected to become increasingly arid over the coming century and that is already burdened by severe geopolitical strife. These records are critical for identifying and quantifying regional climate change patterns, assessing seismic risks, and evaluating the ecological impacts of these processes.

abstract

ABSTRACT Lake Woods, Jurrkulu, is northern Australia's largest freshwater lake filled today in strong Indo–Australian summer monsoons (IASM) to maximum depths of ~5 m. On the western margins of the lake, two large arcuate ridges occur 20 to 40 m above the lake floor and inset within these are other mid‐elevation lake deposits, such as depositional ridges, which suggest past filling events much higher than the modern maximum. We have undertaken the analysis of 20 samples for single‐grain optically stimulated luminescence (OSL) from shoreline and lake‐floor deposits and supplemented this chronology with an analysis of the basin hypsometry, basin‐wide erosion rates and rare earth elements to ascertain the dominant source of the large source‐bordering ridges. The outer and inner arcuate ridges represent both aeolian accumulation and inferred lake‐level highstands due to the presence of fine gravel facies and gastropods contained within them. Bayesian Synchronization of Proxy Paleorecords with Reference Chronologies

Marco A. Aquino‐López, Francesco Muschitiello, Matt Osman
Environmetrics · 2026-10-25
15.0 matched: paleoclimate, proxy, uncertainty quantification, Bayesian
abstract

ABSTRACT Many scientific fields compare two or more noisy time series that integrate the same process but are recorded on different time scales. In paleoclimate studies, for example, proxy measurements are collected versus stratigraphic depth in a climate archive and then converted to calendar time. Synchronizing two proxy records often requires estimating an alignment that maps the depth (or preliminary age) of an input record onto the calendar‐time scale of an absolutely‐dated target record so that corresponding proxy signals line up. Existing alignment approaches are generally optimization‐based and return a single transformation, providing limited formal uncertainty quantification. Here, we introduce BSync, a Bayesian synchronization framework that treats alignments as inference over a monotone time‐mapping function to match an input to a target record. The alignment is expressed as a transformation of the input depth (or age) scale to match the target record, achieved through a link function that locally expands and compresses the input scale. The model is parameterized through interpretable local rate parameters, enabling the specification of priors on deposition times to regularize the alignment toward physically plausible deformations. BSync jointly infers the aligned chronology and provides posterior uncertainty for the time‐warping function and the resulting age scale. In synthetic data experiments and a real‐data case study, BSync yields well‐calibrated credible intervals for the aligned time scale and achieves more accurate alignments than a state‐of‐the‐art automated method, particularly when independent age constraints are sparse.

Reduced Evapotranspiration Decreases Land Precipitation Despite Enhanced Moisture Convergence: Insights from Perturbed Parameter Ensembles of Earth System Models

Journal of Climate · 2026-10-26
15.0 matched: Earth system model, Earth system, radiative forcing, ensemble, climate change
abstract

Abstract Both canonical theory and climate model simulations predict an increase in globally averaged precipitation with radiatively-forced warming. However, terrestrial processes control surface water and energy fluxes, and therefore can impact surface temperatures independently of radiative forcing. Here, we investigate the sensitivity of the global hydrological cycle to warming induced by changes in land surface conditions using perturbed parameter ensembles from two climate models, which sample a broad range of evapotranspiration (ET) states through modifications to land model parameters. The parameters are perturbed within the range of observationally constrained uncertainty and alter ET through different land surface pathways. We find that reduced ET drives warming yet decreases land-mean precipitation, which we link to strong decreases in near-surface relative humidity from warming. These decreases in relative humidity overcome increases in specific humidity and moisture convergence over land, which are also caused by shifts in large-scale circulation. We explain the tropical precipitation response to ET forcing with a simple scaling that relates changes in the land-ocean contrast in relative humidity to land-mean precipitation change. Further, the suppressed ET induces a shortwave cloud response that amplifies warming. Our results highlight that changes to land surface properties at the magnitude of parametric uncertainty influence warming and precipitation. ET-driven warming reduces land precipitation, opposite in sign to the precipitation increase from radiatively-forced warming. This response is not directly captured by scalings of the global hydrological cycle with radiative forcing. We therefore establish the potential significance of terrestrial ecosystem functioning, and its response to climate change, for hydroclimate.

abstract

Abstract The environment in which a glacier terminates can strongly influence its behaviour, stability and future evolution, yet the relationship between terminus type and glacier dynamics at the global scale remains poorly constrained. Here we present a globally-complete classification of glacier termini distinguishing between land, lake, marine and shelf environments, and integrate it with datasets of glacier geometry, dynamics and climate. Chukwuemeka John

abstract

This study examines how climate change is reshaping the stability of glacier fed river systems and the implications for water security in vulnerable regions. Glaciers function as natural water storage, moderating seasonal and interannual variability, yet rising temperatures and altered precipitation regimes are accelerating glacier retreat worldwide. Using an integrated methodological framework combining climate data, glacier mass balance modelling, hydrological simulation, and water security indicators, the study assesses observed and projected changes in river flow magnitude, timing, variability, and extremes across glacier dependent basins. Results show widespread and persistent glacier mass loss, with short term increases in meltwater runoff followed by long term declines in river discharge as ice reserves diminish. Seasonal peak flows are shifting earlier in the year, while late season low flows are becoming more pronounced, reducing flow reliability during periods of high demand. Increased interannual variability and more frequent hydrological extremes further undermine river system stability. These changes translate directly into declining water availability, reduced predictability, and heightened risk for agriculture, hydropower generation, domestic supply, and aquatic ecosystems. The adjusted continuous ranked probability score (aCRPS) is a fair and ensemble-size-unbiased scoring rule when applied to ensembles with forecast members that are interpretable as conditionally independent draws from an underlying predictive distribution.
