abstract

An assumption commonly used for ice streams in ice sheet models is that the vertical profile of horizontal velocity is constant throughout the ice column from the surface to the base.Here, we use airborne radar data for mass flux balance analysis of the central part of the Northeast Greenland Ice Stream, to ascertain whether this assumption is broadly applicable in the ice stream's central region where the ice flow appears to be channelled by a large subglacial trough.We investigate the along-flow evolution of mass flux balance of both the full ice thickness and three englacial units in steady-state, and analyse the spatial variation of their imbalances.We assess the potential over-and under-estimation of ice flow velocity within the ice column and infer vertical shear profile models for our study region.These inferences suggest that the ice stream in this central region is flowing as a block, or plugflow, rather than with deformation from internal shearing, despite large variations in bed topography, basal shear stresses, and in a region with relatively slow ice flow velocities.

Permafrost carbon–climate feedback amplifies Earth system tipping risks

Norman Julius Steinert, Gregory Munday, Marit Sandstad, Benjamin Mark Sanderson, Nico Wunderling
Environmental Research Letters · 2026-06-12 · cites-me
55.0 matched: ice sheet, Antarctic, Greenland, West Antarctic, tipping point, tipping element, overshoot, Atlantic meridional overturning circulation, overturning circulation, permafrost, Earth system, climate feedback, climate change, global warming
abstract

Abstract Global warming leads to widespread permafrost thaw and subsequent emissions of carbon dioxide and methane, driving the permafrost carbon–climate feedback (PCF) which amplifies climate change. Many current-generation climate models omit this feedback, limiting projections of long-term temperature outcomes and associated Earth system tipping risks. Here, we investigate how this feedback affects the risk and timing of crossing tipping points in the Earth system by integrating permafrost carbon emissions into temperature projections for idealized stabilization and overshoot scenarios. In a conceptual network model of interacting climate tipping elements—including the Greenland and West Antarctic ice sheets, the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation, and the Amazon rainforest, we find that PCF leads to additional warming that increases the probability of exceeding one or more tipping elements by up to 50% and can substantially accelerate the timing of tipping events by hundreds of years. The effect of PCF on tipping risk and acceleration is particularly evident in overshoot scenarios with lower peak temperatures below 2–3 ∘ C or peak cumulative emissions of below 2000 PgC. Considering PCF, therefore, narrows the critical temperature space for overshoot pathways. Our results highlight that failing to account for PCF underestimates both climate risks and the urgency of mitigation, increasing the likelihood of tipping events across the Earth system.

abstract

The Antarctic Ice Sheet (AIS) plays a major role in the global climate, providing a natural laboratory to study the processes that interlink the cryosphere, solid Earth, atmosphere, and hydrosphere. Through its ongoing mass loss, the AIS currently contributes to global-mean sea-level rise at a rate of 0.4 mm a −1 . However, its impact on and feedback with climate and sea-level changes are still poorly understood. To generate reliable sea-level projections for the future, there is an urgent need to substantially improve our knowledge of the solid-Earth properties and subsequent response to ice-mass variations in space and time. This issue has been identified as a key question by the international community, strongly supported by the Scientific Committee on Antarctic Research (SCAR). We review how state-of-the-art Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS) and seismic networks in Antarctica, in combination with further ground-based, airborne and satellite-based geophysical measurements, have been used to significantly advance our understanding of solid-Earth processes and their impact on the cryosphere. Future research activities should be coordinated to answer the most urgent science questions. For example, how can we consistently test and model, respectively, a realistic mantle rheology that includes lateral inhomogeneity and transient relaxation, together with AIS mass changes on all time scales since the Last Glacial Maximum, including Holocene retreat and readvance? What processes and boundary conditions should coupled ice-sheet/gravity-rotation-deformation models include in order to realistically reflect the complex interaction between the solid Earth, ice sheet and ocean? Based on the recent achievements and the most urgent science questions, a vision is articulated for science-driven, multidisciplinary geophysical observations and corresponding technological developments. Long-term observations and improved modelling must be combined to develop precise and reliable projections for Antarctica's future mass change and associated contribution to sea-level change at both regional and global scales.

33.0 matched: ice sheet, subglacial, subglacial hydrology, ice dynamics, Greenland, basal melt, mass loss
abstract

Abstract Changes in ice surface elevation and slope influence subglacial water pressure and sliding velocity. Leveraging changes in elevation observed by the Ice, Cloud and land-Elevation satellite (ICESat) and ICESat-2 missions, we use coupled subglacial hydrology–ice dynamics modeling applied to a section of the northwest Greenland ice sheet to explore (i) how decadal-scale changes in ice surface elevation influence changes in winter background subglacial hydrology and corresponding ice velocity, and (ii) how much seasonal response is attributable to geometry versus environmental forcing. This work demonstrates feedbacks that influence mass loss from the Greenland ice sheet as surface elevation evolves, highlighting the subtleties involved in accounting for changes in effective pressure, ice velocity and basal melt due to changes in subglacial hydrology.

abstract

Abstract Eskers are used to characterize glacial drainage systems to inform ice-sheet modeling. However, the connection between eskers preserved in the paleoglaciologic record and the conditions required to deposit them is not well understood, particularly for englacial eskers. We use uncrewed aerial vehicle data over weekly-to-annual timescales from 2021 to 2024 at Breiðamerkurjökull in Iceland to investigate how englacial eskers evolve after emerging from the glacier. During the study period, an esker emerged from an englacial conduit, experienced significant modification and was emplaced on the outwash plain at the edge of a proglacial lake. abstract

Abstract The El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) is the dominant mode of interannual variability in the climate system, altering global circulation patterns and causing changes in temperature and precipitation in remote regions through teleconnections. Because ecosystems are sensitive to climate variations, ENSO teleconnections have large implications for the magnitude of carbon uptake by terrestrial vegetation at interannual timescales. Using ten CMIP6 Earth System Models during 1950–2014, we find global carbon uptake is reduced 0.68± 0.18 PgC per °C increase in the Nino3.4 sea surface temperature, primarily due to reduced gross primary productivity (GPP), with a secondary contribution from increased heterotrophic respiration. We quantify the ENSO impact on GPP and identify several key regions in the tropics (Amazon, Southeast Asia) and extratropics (Australia, Northwest North America) where the ensemble shows regionally coherent, statistically significant changes to GPP. In the Amazon, most models suppress GPP due to increased temperature, but three models exhibit a weaker reduction or slight enhancement in GPP. Inter-model differences in background soil moisture state mediate the relative importance of temperature compared to radiation in controlling total and ENSO-driven GPP variability in the Amazon, with drier models exhibiting a stronger temperature-driven response and tending to exhibit greater reductions in GPP associated with ENSO. Our results suggest that, across the ensemble, land model sensitivities to simulated physical climate anomalies play a large role in the GPP response to ENSO conditions and that these responses differ regionally even within the same latitude range.

Abstract Whether extreme climate events reflect intrinsic threshold behaviour of the climate system remains uncertain, in part because most terrestrial archives integrate multiple climatic signals and lack simultaneous constraints on temperature and hydroclimate.
