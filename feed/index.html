Centennial-scale intensifications of the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation during Heinrich Stadial 1

Partha Sarathi Jena, Cristiano Mazur Chiessi, Ines Beese, Martin Butzin A, Bruna Borba Dias, Marília de Carvalho Campos, … (+2)
Nature Communications · 2026-05-19 · cites-me new
49.0 matched: Atlantic meridional overturning circulation, AMOC, overturning circulation, Southern Ocean, deglaciation, millennial-scale, proxy, sediment core, radiocarbon, CO2
abstract

Centennial-scale changes in Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) strength might disturb global climate by altering interhemispheric heat transport and CO2 partitioning between the ocean and the atmosphere. Due to the short instrumental record and lack of high-resolution paleo records that so far only resolve millennial-scale changes, centennial-scale changes remain elusive. Here we use radiocarbon ventilation ages from a western equatorial Atlantic sediment core with a remarkably high sedimentation rate to reconstruct AMOC variability during the last deglaciation, with a focus on Heinrich Stadial 1 (HS1; 17.8-14.8 ka). Results from model simulations indicate that ventilation ages serve as a sensitive proxy for AMOC variability under weak overturning conditions (i.e., during HS1) at our study location. Notably, within an overall weakened AMOC condition during HS1, our record shows two centennial-scale AMOC intensifications: one from 16.5 to 15.8 ka and another at ~15.4 ka. These centennial-scale episodes of intensified AMOC briefly revitalized Atlantic interhemispheric heat transport during HS1, resulting in decreased precipitation over northeastern Brazil and short-lived but intense changes in climate elsewhere. These episodes of AMOC intensification likely transported substantial volumes of CO2-rich water from the mid-depth Atlantic to the Southern Ocean, where the CO2 was rapidly outgassed to the atmosphere. Using radiocarbon ages from Atlantic sediments, AMOC variability during Heinrich Stadial 1 is reconstructed, revealing brief intensifications that altered heat transport, reduced rainfall over northeastern Brazil, and likely enhanced atmospheric CO2.

abstract

Abstract Sea-level reconstructions and numerical models suggest that the vulnerable West Antarctic Ice Sheet underwent major mass losses during the late Pleistocene, raising global sea level by several metres. However, direct evidence from ice-sheet proximal records for such retreat has remained inconclusive. Here we investigate variations in clay mineral assemblages and past ocean temperatures archived in West Antarctic continental margin sediments during the last 800,000 years. Our results demonstrate that the supply of glacigenic detritus sourced from the main outlet glaciers in the Amundsen Sea sector of the West Antarctic Ice Sheet ceased almost entirely during all previous warm periods, documenting the loss of their drainage basins. Concomitant seawater warming, revealed by a microfossil proxy, is attributed to intensified deep-water advection towards Antarctica’s Pacific margin, which is corroborated by the clay mineralogical evidence. Our findings show that ocean forcing can drive major deglaciation of West Antarctica and related global sea-level rise.

Cascading impacts of Atlantic meridional overturning circulation weakening and collapse on marine biogeochemistry

Paul E. Lerner, Anastasia Romanou
Communications Earth & Environment · 2026-11-04 · cites-me
46.0 matched: Arctic, tipping point, bifurcation, Atlantic meridional overturning circulation, AMOC, overturning circulation, Earth system model, Earth system, ensemble
abstract

Abstract As anthropogenic emissions continue to increase Earth’s surface temperature, the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) is at risk of weakening substantially and crossing a tipping point. Then, multiple Earth system components may be significantly and irreversibly altered. Here we investigate the responses of ocean carbon uptake, net primary productivity (NPP), oxygen concentration, and carbon storage to an AMOC collapse induced by extremes in the internal variability in the subpolar gyre under a moderate forcing scenario. While similar ensemble splitting has been found in other models, the NASA Goddard Institute for Space Studies (GISS) Earth system model (ESM) was the first to report an AMOC bifurcation due to internal variability, without the need to prescribe freshwater hosing. We find severe regional impacts, including collapsed NPP and deoxygenation in the subpolar North Atlantic, and reduced carbon uptake in the Arctic and subtropical North Atlantic. We identify the physical drivers of these ocean biogeochemical impacts and, for the first time, provide estimates of their lag and latency, i.e. the timescales over which these impacts begin to occur and are fully realized, respectively. Timescales are generally found to increase southward from the deep convection sites, but are remarkably similar between basins at similar latitudes.

Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences · 2026-11-09 new
41.0 matched: Greenland, abrupt change, Eemian, Dansgaard-Oeschger, millennial-scale
abstract

Greenland ice-core records show an approximately 15-fold increase in dust concentration during cold stadial periods compared to warmer interstadial periods in the last glacial period (LGP). While multiple regions including East Asian deserts and the Sahara have been suggested as potential source areas (PSAs) for Greenland dust, millennial-scale variability of their relative contributions associated with Dansgaard–Oeschger climatic fluctuations during the LGP remains poorly constrained. To address this, we investigate high-resolution (~800 y) Pb and Sr isotopic, mineralogical, and geochemical records from the North Greenland Eemian Ice Drilling ice-core samples covering the entire LGP. We found that the Pb and Sr isotopic compositions, kaolinite-to-chlorite ratios, and chemical index of alteration values were higher during stadials than during interstadials. Comparison with PSAs suggests that East Asian deserts remained the dominant contributors to Greenland dust throughout the LGP, but contributions from the Sahara increased during stadial intervals. This shift likely reflects climate-driven changes in atmospheric circulation patterns responsible for transporting dust to Greenland. Our findings provide insights into the relationship between dust cycle dynamics and abrupt climate changes on millennial timescales during the LGP.

Evaluating ice shelf basal melt rates in the Southern Ocean high-resolution model against satellite remote sensing

Andy Dinh, Eric J. Rignot, Matthew R. Mazloff, Ian G. Fenty
Frontiers in Marine Science · 2026-11-06
40.0 matched: ice sheet, ice shelf, grounding line, Antarctica, basal melt, Southern Ocean, sea level, sea-level rise, remote sensing
abstract

Ice shelf melt rates by a warm ocean have a critical impact on ice sheet mass balance in Antarctica and in turn on the rates of sea level rise. Validating and tuning modeled ice shelf melt rates is a challenge. Here we discuss the issues by presenting validation of melt rates calculated from the Southern Ocean high-resolution model (SOhi) over the year 2007. SOhi includes full physics, tides, and an updated bathymetric compilation. We compare the model results to two satellite-derived estimates. Overall, we find that the model overestimates ice shelf melt, which we attribute to the ocean model initial (warm) conditions in some areas, bathymetric uncertainties in others, simplified physics on some ice shelves, and also to the inherent limitations of the satellite-derived estimates. The SOhi model nevertheless provides a comprehensive, detailed mapping of ice shelf melt rates around Antarctica; it captures higher melt near grounding lines and ice fronts, areas of freezing versus areas of high melt water production. The identified discrepancies between model and satellite-estimates provide guidelines for model improvements, requirements for additional observations of ice shelf cavities, and assembling of calibration/validation datasets to constrain the models.

Earth’s climate oscillations between interglacial and glacial periods have driven significant sea level changes. Journal of Climate · 2026-11-05
37.0 matched: Greenland, Arctic, Atlantic meridional overturning circulation, AMOC, overturning circulation, coupled climate model, model intercomparison, CMIP, radiative forcing, ensemble
abstract

Abstract Growing observational and modeling evidence suggests that the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) has weakened since the 1980s, with reductions in North American and European (NAEU) aerosols identified as a potential contributing factor. Here we use fully coupled climate model experiments to demonstrate that the radiative forcing associated with decreased NAEU aerosols weakens the AMOC by enhancing freshwater transport from the Arctic and subtropical regions into the deep convection region. Reduced aerosol loading induces large-scale atmospheric circulation adjustments, including cyclonic wind stress anomalies over the Arctic Ocean and the subtropical North Atlantic. Over the Arctic Ocean, the cyclonic wind stress anomaly weakens the Beaufort Gyre, releasing freshwater that accumulates north of the Canadian Arctic Archipelago and Greenland. This freshwater is then exported through Baffin Bay into the Labrador Sea, suppressing deep convection there. Simultaneously, enhanced cyclonic wind stress over the subtropical North Atlantic weakens the North Atlantic Current (NAC), reducing the northward transport of saline water and promoting the freshwater accumulation within the subpolar gyre. This freshwater anomaly is then advected toward the Labrador Sea, further suppressing deep convection and weakening the AMOC. Together, our results identify a remotely driven pathway through which reductions in NAEU aerosols weaken the AMOC via wind-driven freshwater advection. The robustness of this mechanism is further supported by the multi-model ensemble mean from the Coupled Model Intercomparison Project Phase 6 (CMIP6) simulations, which exhibits consistent oceanic and atmospheric circulation responses.

Abstract. The vast majority of studies examining the impact of freshwater from ice sheet melting on the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) use climate models that cannot resolve mesoscale ocean processes and do not include an accurate spatio-temporal distribution of the freshwater forcing. These two factors critically affect the nature of the AMOC response. abstract

The stability of the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) is a major uncertainty in projections of future climate. However, direct evidence for AMOC tipping behavior on orbital timescales remains scarce. Here, we reconstruct the strength of Iceland-Scotland Overflow Water, a key lower branch of the deep Atlantic circulation, over the past 800,000 years by combining 12 current-speed records from North Atlantic sediment cores with a data-driven hybrid model. The resulting reconstruction reveals persistent alternation between weak (glacial) and strong (interglacial) circulation modes, pervasive millennial-scale variability within both regimes, and systematic asymmetry between weak-to-strong recoveries and strong-to-weak collapses. Threshold and phase-space analyses show that regime shifts occur within a narrow window of atmospheric carbon dioxide and sea level and often follow hysteresis-like loops. Although bistability persists throughout the last 800,000 years, its sensitivity and timing evolve through time. These findings provide long-term, high-resolution, data-driven constraints on the bistability of Nordic Seas overflow waters and its implications for AMOC resilience in a warming world.

abstract

Dansgaard-Oeschger (DO) events during the last ice age are thought to reflect rapid shifts in the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC). Greenland ice cores suggest warming transitions within only a few decades—or even just over 10 years. Using precisely dated speleothem oxygen-isotope records from the Asian monsoon region, we have shown that the same DO warmings unfolded over more than a century. abstract

ABSTRACT Understanding Earth‐surface processes along the fringes of large ice sheets is integral to deciphering cold‐region environmental variability and anticipating slope instability under climate change. We investigate the Meseta Chile Chico, a volcanic plateau in the central Patagonian Andes, located at the eastern margin of the former Patagonian Ice Sheet. Here, glacier retreat patterns are well‐constrained, while the influence of periglacial, paraglacial, and postglacial processes is not. We employ geomorphological mapping integrated with geological and palaeoclimate reconstructions to provide a spatio‐temporal model of landscape evolution. Enhanced erosion since the late Pliocene, caused by differential tectonic uplift and glacial isostatic rebound, produced the rugged morphology that characterizes the Meseta Chile Chico. The geomorphology of the Meseta Chile Chico reflects an interplay between inherited geology, (de)glaciation, and climate, highlighting the importance of an interdisciplinary approach for understanding recent landscape dynamics in deglaciating mountain regions.

abstract

Abstract. An external perturbation to the climate system from anthropogenic or natural activity first impacts the climate by inducing a perturbation to Earth’s energy budget, known as a radiative forcing. The characteristics of the radiative forcing, such as its global-mean magnitude and spatial pattern, determine the subsequent climate response. Therefore, forming accurate projections of climate change first requires diagnosing radiative forcing and evaluating its persistent uncertainty in Global Climate Models. As part of the Coupled Model Intercomparison Project phase 7 (CMIP7), the second iteration of the Radiative Forcing Model Intercomparison Project (RFMIP2.0) will enable the systematic characterization of effective radiative forcing and its components across state-of-the-art climate models, through a set of fixed-Sea Surface Temperature timeslice and transient experiments. RFMIP2.0 also includes new experiments that will address open questions about the definition of radiative forcing, such as its sensitivity to evolving surface conditions, and will further enhance an ever-growing swath of science applications that rely on an understanding of Earth’s energy budget.
